# Offline meal photo model decision — refreshed October 24, 2026

The Challenge candidate must stay fully offline and below the size limit in Apple's currently published [2026 terms](https://developer.apple.com/swift-student-challenge/policy/). A food name from a photo is a clue to confirm, never a source for recipe, portion, carbohydrate grams, or insulin units. We will not ship a model solely because it recognizes the three bundled examples.

## Implemented path and open evidence

The earlier October 24 classification Guide/instructions matched a small reviewed native Mac comparison that completed the previously failing meals and pet controls. Subsequent source-derived iOS 27.1 checks used unchanged processing code from historical ZIP `6457bc79…` and commit `c49c209`, in a separate bundle with the model available. One bundled synthetic image produced tentative names, zero amount components, no total and no review readiness. Unsupported-locale and pre-cancelled-entry guards and actual local-store save/read/delete, fixed user-entry provenance and backup exclusion were observed; physical file protection remains unverified. See the [historical iOS report](research/foundation-models/ios-simulator-20261024.json) and [central evidence](research/foundation-models/README.md). These are not runs of the newly applied prompt and do not establish the cause of earlier guardrail errors.

Five [additional historical iOS matrix scopes](research/foundation-models/ios-diagnostics-20261024.json) checked direct zero/four-image entry bounds; four procedural image-preparation cases (up/left-mirrored, scales 1/2, 768-pixel bound and effective corner/aspect preservation); three views of one bundled synthetic meal with no generated amount; one procedural nonfood pattern with an inspected-empty result; and cancellation after an external entry witness followed by one awaited recovery request. The bounds are direct-inspector checks, preparation covers those fixtures only, and the cancellation witness does not prove internal generation began or OS computation stopped. No deadline or calibrated latency claim follows.

A new frozen collection of six varied Commons meals plus empty mugs and shoes then received one request per image under that historical prompt. Visual review found useful broad clues alongside unsupported `garlic` in soup/tacos, unsupported distinct sandwich `cheese`, ambiguous taco `red pepper`, and duplicated `green onions`/`scallions`. Unsupported specificity is not proof that an ingredient is absent. Both nonfood controls completed with empty accepted lists; an unavailable/error result would not count as correct abstention. The lawful images and their source/rights/expectation records remain outside the app and ZIP; repository evidence records provenance without redistributing pixels.

The current source now uses the exact tested `main-visible-food-v1` text, SHA-256 `143feea02ca5918360904cdbbfc4f17d8ea6acd87eda8edf2e4f2ba60ee0290c`. Only the names Guide, system instructions and request text changed; the schema, six-name maximum, local model, default Apple guardrails, generation options, structural gates, display policy and amount separation remain unchanged. The [recorded proposal](research/foundation-models/main-visible-food-20261024.json) and [review](research/foundation-models/main-visible-food-20261024-review.md) retain one fixed request for each of the same eight exposed images. Observed outputs became `soup`, `tacos` and `sandwich`, avoiding the earlier unsupported ingredients and duplicate in those calls. The bread clue changed to `flatbread`, with a still-generic `sauce` clue. Pasta still returned `noodles`, `meat`, `vegetables` and `sauce` despite the whole-dish preference; plantain identity remains tentative. This is a bounded usability decision from tuning observations, not a reliable or causal improvement claim.

Three separately frozen scope cases, selected without proposal answers, returned `apple`, `cake` and `milk`. They show that these particular snack, dessert and visibly filled drink examples remained within scope; there are no baseline calls on them. `Milk` is a compatible appearance clue for the white liquid, not verified dairy/plant source, fat content, sweetening, volume or nutrition. All eleven proposal reports retained zero amount components, zero included components, no automatic total and no review readiness. The collections are purposive exploratory diagnostics, not qualified held-out acceptance, worldwide-food accuracy or clinical validation.

The name policy bounds display text, not correctness. Names stay separate from carbohydrate items and only prefill an editable name; a checked amount is still required. Independent recognition/abstention, representative performance and physical offline/network-isolation checks remain open. Historical `6457bc79…` source/exact-ZIP builds and initial Home rendering do not prove the newly packaged prompt's build or full journey. Consult [readiness](CHALLENGE-READINESS.md) for final artifact identity, packaging and exact-build results; no new package build result is asserted here. Separate processing runs do not prove full exact-app model/clue or fallback interactions, camera/label journeys or accessibility. Testing only: build 2 remains the last verified internal Testing build, builds 3 and 4 uploaded successfully for internal testing only with processing/compliance/group verification pending, and no App Store/external/public release is authorized.

The UECFoodPix benchmark is research-only; its provider permits non-commercial research and its images are not redistributed. Its Japanese and mixed-dish categories improve the evaluation breadth, but they still do not cover meals from everywhere. The next model experiment needs a lawful set of real, diverse home, school, restaurant, mixed-dish, packaged, and nonfood photos, with consent and known labels. Measure top suggestions, abstention, false food matches, latency, device support, and final ZIP size. Keep a manual path for every meal. Until that evidence exists, the app must say when photo inspection is unavailable or uncertain and let the person use a trusted carbohydrate source.
